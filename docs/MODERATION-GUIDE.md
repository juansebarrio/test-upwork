# Moderating Tea Talks

A short guide for whoever reads the stories. No technical knowledge needed.

## Signing in

1. Open `https://tea.example.org/admin` in your browser (the real address will be in your welcome note).
2. Type the moderator password and press **Sign in**.
3. Signing in takes about half a second on purpose; that is normal.
4. You are logged out automatically after 30 minutes without activity, and when you close the browser. Press **Log out** (at the right end of the Waiting / Approved row) when you finish, especially on a shared computer.

If the password does not work, nothing bad has happened: just try again. Nobody is counting attempts, and nothing is recorded.

## What you see

The **Waiting** tab lists new stories, with a count. Each one shows the text and the day it arrived ("Received today", "Received yesterday", or the date). That is genuinely all there is. There is no name, no email, no location, no time of day. If someone asks you "who sent this?", the honest answer is "there is no way to know, and we built it that way on purpose".

The **Approved** tab lists stories you have already accepted.

## The buttons

**Approve** means "this can be shared". If the public archive is switched on, the story appears on the Stories page straight away, showing only the text and the month it arrived. If the archive is off, approving just keeps the story for later. The line at the bottom of the panel tells you which of the two is the case.

**Delete** removes the story from the database completely. The card asks once, right there, "Delete this story for good?" with **Keep it** and **Yes, delete it**. After that there is no recycle bin, no undo, and no copy anywhere. This is deliberate: something that no longer exists cannot be leaked or demanded.

**Unpublish**, on the Approved tab, moves a story back to Waiting. It disappears from the Stories page immediately. From Waiting you can then delete it if you change your mind about it.

## What to look for before approving

Read each story with one question in mind: **could this text point to a real person?** Even without a name, a combination of small details can identify someone in a small community. Look out for:

- names, nicknames, initials
- workplaces, schools, universities, specific shops or cafés
- street names, neighbourhoods, villages, distinctive landmarks
- exact dates ("on 14 March") or unusual events that were in the news
- job titles that only one or two people hold
- descriptions of another person that could identify them, including family members or partners

If a story is beautiful but contains a detail like that, the safest choice is to delete it. You cannot edit stories in the panel, and quietly editing someone's words is not something we do. If the collective wants to invite the person to resubmit a safer version, that has to happen through your usual channels, because there is no way to contact the author.

Spam and junk also arrive sometimes. Delete it. Nothing about it is kept.

## Things that keep everyone safer

- Sign in from a device you trust. The panel is protected by a password, but the safest panel is one that is only opened from a trusted phone or laptop.
- Do not take screenshots of the panel or copy stories into chats, documents or emails. The moment a story leaves the panel it is somewhere we cannot delete.
- Do not read the panel on a screen others can see over your shoulder.
- Keep the password in a password manager, not in a note or a message thread.
- If the password may have been seen by someone else, tell the person who runs the server. Changing it takes one minute and signs everyone out.

## What we can and cannot tell people

We can say, truthfully:

- "We do not know who you are."
- "We keep only what you wrote and the day it arrived."
- "When we delete something, it is gone."

We cannot promise:

- that a story is safe to publish just because it has no name in it. That is exactly what moderation is for.
- that the internet provider or the phone someone used keeps no trace. Tea Talks controls only its own server. The quick exit button and the "What Tea Talks does not collect" document explain this to visitors.
