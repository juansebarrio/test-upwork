# What Tea Talks does not collect, and why

*A plain-language note from Shai Collective, for anyone thinking about sharing a story.*

Tea Talks is a page where you can write something and pass it to us without telling us who you are. We wanted to explain, honestly and in ordinary words, what happens to what you write, and what does not happen.

## The one idea behind everything

**Information we never collect cannot be leaked, hacked, subpoenaed or demanded from us.**

If someone breaks into our server, they find only what is there. If someone with authority orders us to hand over what we have, we can only hand over what we have. So we built Tea Talks to have as little as possible.

## What we keep

For each story, three things, and nothing more:

1. **The words you wrote.**
2. **Whether a moderator has approved it** for sharing or not yet.
3. **The day it arrived.** Only the date. Not the hour, not the minute.

That is the entire record. Anyone with access to the database can confirm this: there are no other columns to look in.

## What we do not keep, on purpose

- **Your IP address.** Every website is technically shown the address your connection comes from. Ours is set up so that it is never written down anywhere, not by the page and not by the software in front of it.
- **Your device or browser details.** Nothing about which phone, which app or which language you use.
- **Cookies.** The story page sets none. There is nothing on your device afterwards that says you were here.
- **The exact time.** Only the day. A precise time can be matched against other records; a date is shared by everyone who wrote that day.
- **Anything from other companies.** The page loads no fonts, images, scripts or tracking from anyone else. The browser is instructed to refuse such requests even if one were accidentally added later.
- **Login attempts, page views, error details about you.** The server keeps no visitor log at all. This is not a setting we might forget to switch off; the log does not exist.
- **The story on the confirmation screen.** After you send, the page thanks you without showing your words again, so nothing is left on screen if someone walks in.

## What happens to your story

A person from the collective reads it. They approve it for sharing, or they delete it. Deleting is real: the story is removed from the database and the freed space is overwritten. There is no bin, no archive of removed stories, no copy.

If we share stories publicly, we share only the words and the date.

## The quick exit

Every page has a calm **Leave quickly** button, and the Escape key does the same thing. It clears anything you have typed and swaps this page for a weather site, in a way that the Back button will not bring you back here. Use it whenever you like. It is there for you, not for us.

## What we cannot control, said plainly

We control our server and nothing else.

- **Your own device** may keep browsing history or a cached copy of the page. Private browsing mode, or clearing history afterwards, takes care of this.
- **Your internet provider, mobile network, or someone on the same Wi-Fi** can see that a connection was made to our address, though not what was written, because the connection is encrypted. If that matters to you, Tor Browser hides it, and Tea Talks works in Tor Browser.
- **The company that hosts our server** (in Germany) can see traffic going to it at the network level, like any host can. It cannot see the stories.
- **The words themselves.** If you write your name, your workplace or your street, that is in the story. Our moderators watch for this and will delete rather than publish something that could point to a person. Please help them by leaving such details out.

## Why we are telling you all this

Because "trust us" is not enough. We would rather show you exactly where the edges are, so you can decide for yourself what to share. The code of Tea Talks is open, and anyone who knows how can check every claim on this page.
