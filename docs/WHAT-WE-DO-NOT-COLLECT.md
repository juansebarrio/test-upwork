# What Tea Talks does not collect, and why

*A plain-language note from Shai Collective, for anyone thinking about sharing a story or writing to us.*

Tea Talks is a page where you can write something and pass it to us without telling us who you are. The Contact page works the same way. We wanted to explain, honestly and in ordinary words, what happens to what you write, and what does not happen.

## The one idea behind everything

**Information we never collect cannot be leaked, hacked, subpoenaed or demanded from us.**

If someone breaks into our server, they find only what is there. If someone with authority orders us to hand over what we have, we can only hand over what we have. So we built this site to have as little as possible.

## What we keep when you share a story

Three things, and nothing more:

1. **The words you wrote.**
2. **Whether a moderator has published it**, set it aside, or not decided yet.
3. **The day it arrived.** Only the date. Not the hour, not the minute.

That is the entire record. Anyone with access to the database can confirm this: there are no other columns to look in.

## What we keep when you write to us

Also three things: the words you wrote, the day they arrived, and, only if you choose to give one, a way to reply to you. That reply address is erased the moment we have answered, and in any case after 30 days, automatically. The message itself stays only until we delete it.

## What we do not keep, on purpose

- **Your IP address.** Every request on the internet carries an address so it can be routed; what matters is that our server does not record it, and nothing derived from it is stored.
- **Your device or browser details.** Nothing about which phone, which app or which language you use.
- **Cookies.** The public pages set none. There is nothing on your device afterwards that says you were here.
- **The exact time.** Only the day. A precise time can be matched against other records; a date is shared by everyone who wrote that day.
- **Anything from other companies.** The site loads no fonts, images, scripts or tracking from anyone else. The browser is instructed to refuse such requests even if one were accidentally added later.
- **Login attempts, page views, error details about you.** The server keeps no visitor log at all. This is not a setting we might forget to switch off; the log does not exist.
- **Your story on the confirmation screen.** After you send, the page thanks you without showing your words again, so nothing is left on screen if someone walks in.

## What happens to your story

A person from the collective reads it. They publish it, set it aside, or delete it. Sometimes they remove a detail that could identify someone before publishing; when they do, the original wording is gone. Deleting is real: the story is removed from the database and the freed space is overwritten. There is no bin, no archive of removed stories, no copy.

If we share stories publicly, we share only the words and the month they arrived. Not even the day.

If you ever want a story taken down, write to us through the Contact page with a phrase from it. We will remove it without asking who you are, because we could not check anyway.

## The quick exit

Every page has a calm **Leave quickly** button, and pressing the Escape key twice does the same thing (twice, so that one accidental press does not throw away what you were writing). It clears anything you have typed and swaps this page for a weather site, in a way that the Back button will not bring you back here. Use it whenever you like. It is there for you, not for us.

## What we cannot control, said plainly

We control our server and nothing else.

- **Your own device** may keep browsing history or a cached copy of the page. Private browsing mode, or clearing history afterwards, takes care of this.
- **Your internet provider, mobile network, or someone on the same Wi-Fi** can see that a connection was made to our address, though not what was written, because the connection is encrypted. If that matters to you, Tor Browser hides it, and the site works in Tor Browser.
- **The company that hosts our server** can see traffic going to it at the network level, like any host can, and keeps its own records outside our reach. It cannot see the stories. We chose where the site lives for its privacy laws; that is protection, not invisibility.
- **The site's name is public.** When a site goes online, its name appears in public records of security certificates, permanently. Anyone can see that this site has existed since a certain date.
- **The words themselves.** If you write your name, your workplace or your street, that is in the story. Our moderators watch for this and will remove or set aside something that could point to a person. Please help them by leaving such details out.

## Why we are telling you all this

Because "trust us" is not enough. We would rather show you exactly where the edges are, so you can decide for yourself what to share. The code of the site is open, and anyone who knows how can check every claim on this page.

Pour yourself a cup. You are welcome here.

*Shai Collective*
